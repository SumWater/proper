\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

The evidence covers one benchmark, one 100-memory bank, two instruction-tuned 7B/8B models, three constructed failure strata, and one recovery decision. The primary changed-target population measures selector capacity efficiently but is not a random deployment population; the all-valid analysis is a sensitivity check. Results do not establish multi-step or external-benchmark generalization.

Automated Recovery Validity has not yet completed its human reliability audit. \todoVOne. Until then, evaluator reliability is an explicit submission blocker rather than an inferred property.

The Mistral result shows that a selector can improve relative to other memory methods while memory remains worse than No Memory. The contradiction ablation is inconsistent across models. No observed safety events imply neither a tight risk bound nor deployment safety. Finally, subgroup, intervention, and concentration analyses are descriptive and cannot establish causal mechanisms.
